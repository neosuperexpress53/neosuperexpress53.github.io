Summary

Wuthering Heights is Emily Brontë’s haunting tale of passion, vengeance, and the destructive power of unrestrained emotion. Set against the bleak and windswept Yorkshire moors, the novel follows the turbulent bond between Heathcliff and Catherine Earnshaw, a relationship that defies social boundaries and endures beyond death. Through a layered narrative and shifting perspectives, Brontë explores themes of obsession, class, cruelty, and the longing for belonging. Dark, atmospheric, and psychologically complex, the novel remains a masterpiece of English literature and a profound study of human nature.
